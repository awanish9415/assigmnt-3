# Level 11: HR / Behavioral Questions

> Calsoft HR/manager will ask these. Prepare honest, short answers.

---

## SECTION A: About You (1-10)

### Q1. Tell me about yourself.
**Answer (template):**
"Hello, I am [Your Name]. I have [X years] of experience in [telecom / networking / software]. I have worked on [specific area: 4G/5G/IPSec/VPN/etc.]. My recent project was [briefly describe]. I am skilled in [C/C++, Linux, networking protocols]. I am looking for a role where I can grow in 5G RAN/Core development, and Calsoft's work in O-RAN is very exciting for me."

**Tip:** Keep it 60-90 seconds. Cover: education → current role → key skills → why you want this job.

### Q2. Why do you want to join Calsoft?
**Answer:**
"Calsoft is a leader in telecom engineering services, especially in 5G RAN and core. I want to work on cutting-edge O-RAN technology — open fronthaul, srsRAN, Open5GS — which matches my interests. Calsoft's exposure to multiple operator and vendor projects will help me build deep expertise. I also like the engineering culture of solving real, hard problems."

### Q3. Why are you leaving your current company?
**Answer (safe answer):**
"I am looking for opportunities to work more deeply in 5G RAN/Core. My current role had limited exposure to [5G O-RAN / open source 5G core]. I want to grow in this area, and Calsoft offers that. I have learned a lot at my current company and respect the team, but it is time for a new challenge."

**Avoid:** Negative talk about current employer.

### Q4. What are your strengths?
**Answer:**
"My main strengths are:
1. Strong fundamentals in networking and protocols.
2. Hands-on with Linux, C/C++, debugging tools (gdb, Wireshark, tcpdump).
3. Quick learner — I learned [recent technology] in a few weeks.
4. Good at troubleshooting — I enjoy finding root cause."

### Q5. What are your weaknesses?
**Answer:**
"One area I am improving is [pick something honest but not critical]:
- 'PHY layer details — I am stronger at L2/L3, so I have been reading more about 5G NR PHY.'
- 'Public speaking — I am working on it by leading team meetings.'

I see it as an opportunity to grow."

### Q6. Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
**Answer:**
"In 5 years, I want to be a technical lead or senior engineer in 5G RAN/Core, owning end-to-end design and delivery of features. I want to be the go-to person for [specific area, e.g., O-RAN fronthaul]. I also want to mentor juniors."

### Q7. What are your salary expectations?
**Answer:**
"Based on my experience and market standards, I am expecting around [X LPA]. But I am flexible and want to look at the total package — role, learning, and growth. I am sure we can come to a fair number."

**Tip:** Research market rate first. Ask range.

### Q8. When can you join?
**Answer:**
"My current notice period is [30/60/90 days]. I will try my best to negotiate early release. Realistically, I can join in [X weeks]. If you have flexibility, I can also work on transitioning faster."

### Q9. Do you have any other offers?
**Answer:**
"Yes, I am in discussion with [1-2] other companies, but Calsoft is my top preference because of [specific reason — O-RAN focus, engineering team, etc.]." OR "I am actively looking and have a few interviews lined up."

**Be honest. Don't bluff.**

### Q10. What do you know about Calsoft?
**Answer:**
"Calsoft is a 25+ year old technology engineering services company based in Pune, focused on:
- Telecom / 5G / O-RAN
- Cloud & virtualization
- Storage & data
- AI/ML

I have read that Calsoft works on O-RAN open fronthaul, 5G core, and contributes to test frameworks. The role I am interviewing for matches my goal to work deep in 5G."

## SECTION B: Behavioral / Situational (11-25)

### Q11. Tell me about a difficult bug you solved.
**Answer (STAR format):**
- **Situation:** "In a previous project, we had random crashes in our [Linux/networking] module under load."
- **Task:** "I was the lead engineer for that module."
- **Action:** "I enabled core dumps, used gdb to find the stack trace. I noticed a race condition in shared queue. I added proper locking and tested with thread sanitizer (TSAN)."
- **Result:** "The crash was fixed. We added unit tests to prevent regression."

### Q12. Tell me about a project you are most proud of.
**Answer:**
- Pick a real project.
- Explain your role specifically.
- Mention technical highlights (protocols, tools).
- Outcome (delivered on time, performance gain, customer happy).

### Q13. How do you handle tight deadlines?
**Answer:**
"I break the work into small tasks, prioritize by impact, and communicate progress daily. If I see risk of slipping, I raise it early so we can re-plan. I also focus deeply during deadline crunch by limiting context-switching."

### Q14. Tell me about a conflict with a colleague. How did you handle it?
**Answer:**
"In one case, a team member and I disagreed on a design approach — they wanted [X], I wanted [Y]. We spent an hour discussing pros and cons. We ended up combining the ideas. I focus on the technical merit, not personal egos."

### Q15. What if you don't agree with your manager's decision?
**Answer:**
"I will share my concerns clearly with data and reasoning in a 1:1 conversation. If they still decide differently, I will support the decision and execute, because they may have context I don't. After execution, we can review and learn together."

### Q16. Tell me about a time you failed.
**Answer:**
"In a previous release, I underestimated the testing time for a complex feature. We hit issues late and missed our deadline by a week. I learned to be more conservative in time estimates and to do incremental integration."

### Q17. How do you stay updated with new technology?
**Answer:**
"I follow:
- 3GPP releases and standards on 3gpp.org.
- O-RAN Alliance documents.
- GitHub repos (srsRAN, Open5GS, free5GC).
- Conference talks (ONS, MWC).
- Blogs (Nick van Snetworking, Sukchan Lee).
- LinkedIn engineers in the space."

### Q18. Are you comfortable working in a team / individually?
**Answer:**
"Both. I love collaborative problem-solving — pair programming, design discussions — but I can also work independently when I have clear requirements and goals."

### Q19. How do you prioritize when you have multiple tasks?
**Answer:**
"I look at:
1. Urgency (deadline).
2. Impact (customer / team).
3. Dependencies (am I blocking others?).
I make a list, get manager alignment on top 2-3, and focus."

### Q20. Have you led a team?
**Answer (if yes):** "Yes, I led a team of [X] engineers on [project]. I handled task allocation, code reviews, design discussions, and mentoring."
**(If no):** "Not officially, but I have mentored juniors and led technical discussions. I am ready to take on lead responsibilities."

### Q21. How do you mentor juniors?
**Answer:**
"By pair programming, code reviews with detailed comments, and giving them ownership of features with my support. I share resources (docs, books) and encourage them to ask questions. I also do regular 1:1s."

### Q22. What kind of work environment do you prefer?
**Answer:**
"Open, collaborative, where engineers can challenge each other respectfully. I like clear goals but freedom to choose how to solve. Mix of focus time and collaboration."

### Q23. Tell me about a time you went above and beyond.
**Answer:**
"Before a major release, we had a critical bug found on Friday evening. I stayed back, debugged the issue, root caused it as a memory corruption, fixed and tested on weekend. Release went out on time."

### Q24. How do you handle pressure?
**Answer:**
"I focus on what I can control. I break the problem into smaller pieces, communicate with team, ask for help when stuck, and take small breaks to stay clear-headed."

### Q25. Why should we hire you?
**Answer:**
"I have the right mix of:
1. Networking and telecom fundamentals.
2. Hands-on C/C++ and Linux skills.
3. Curiosity and quick learning — I have explored srsRAN/Open5GS [or whatever you've explored].
4. Team-friendly attitude.

I am genuinely interested in 5G O-RAN, and I will bring focus and dedication to Calsoft."

## SECTION C: Questions YOU Should Ask Interviewer (26-30)

> At the end, interviewer asks "Do you have any questions?" — ALWAYS say yes. It shows interest.

### Q26-30. Good questions to ask:

1. **"Can you tell me more about the team I'll be working with and the current projects?"**
2. **"What does success look like in the first 6 months for this role?"**
3. **"How does Calsoft contribute to open source projects like srsRAN or Open5GS?"**
4. **"What's the team's involvement in O-RAN Alliance work?"**
5. **"What learning and certification support does Calsoft provide?"**
6. **"How is the transition from L1 to next interview rounds, and what should I prepare?"**
7. **"What are the biggest technical challenges the team is facing right now?"**

**Avoid:**
- Don't ask about leave policy, work hours, perks in L1.
- Don't ask anything answerable on Google ("What does Calsoft do?").
